### Area
Other

### Is this evidence or preference?
Evidence — something was actually lost

### What happened
`docs/REVIEW_RUBRIC.md` scores as a conjunction: one failed criterion rejects. In
practice this means a proposal with overwhelming evidence, perfect generality and a
tiny blast radius is rejected outright for a thin self-criticism section — a section
that is the easiest of all eight to repair. The rejection is correct by the rubric
and wasteful in outcome, because the resubmission differs only in one paragraph.

### What it cost
Two proposals rejected and never resubmitted, both failing on a single repairable
criterion while passing the seven that are hard to fake.

### How often
A few times

### Rounds run
18

### Proposed change
Split the criteria into blocking and repairable:

> Criteria 1, 2, 3 and 5 remain blocking — a failure is a rejection. Criteria 4, 6,
> 7 and 8 become *request changes*: the reviewer names what is missing and leaves
> the issue open for one revision.

This preserves the reject-by-default posture on everything expensive to fake, and
stops discarding good findings over paragraphs.

### Blast radius
Submitters get a revision path on four criteria. The reviewer does one more pass on
some proposals.

### Why this might be wrong
A conjunction is what stops a strong section buying a weak one, and any split
reintroduces that trade through the back door — "repairable" becomes "optional" the
moment a reviewer is busy. The rubric explicitly argues for the conjunction and this
proposal is arguing against a deliberate design decision, not an oversight.
